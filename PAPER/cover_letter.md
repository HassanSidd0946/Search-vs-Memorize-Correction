Dear Editors of *NeuroImage: Reports*,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"A Mislabeled Contrast, Recovered: Diagnosing and Correcting an Encode/Test-Phase Confound in Blocked EEG Decoding,"** for consideration as a research article.

We believe *NeuroImage: Reports* is the right venue for this work. The journal's scope explicitly welcomes papers on methods, models, and reproducibility-focused contributions, which matches this paper's central aim: a composition-arithmetic diagnostic protocol for detecting label-dictionary confounds in blocked, multi-phase EEG paradigms, generalizable beyond the single dataset examined here. The paper is structured as a self-audit of our own analysis pipeline rather than a conventional hypothesis-confirmation study, and we believe the journal's open, methods-oriented format suits that framing well.

Briefly: we found that our own class-label dictionary for a Search-vs-Memorize EEG decoding contrast conflated four structurally distinct marker types, producing an originally reported 70.78% headline accuracy that was arithmetically correct but scientifically uninterpretable as a claim about task decoding. We diagnosed and confirmed this error using a composition-arithmetic technique and a pre-registered joint criterion, formally withdrawing our own prior mechanistic account for the same contrast. Correcting the class definition to encode-phase trials only recovers a real, modest, cross-subject pre-calibration signal (0.5737), validated by four independent robustness checks, with the underlying diagnostic protocol offered as a reusable, generalizable contribution.

All analytical thresholds were pre-registered before the corresponding analyses were run, and the full pre-registration record is available for review. The source dataset (ds005189, OpenNeuro, CC0 license) is fully open, and our analysis code will be made available in a public repository, with a specific commit tagged at submission.
